How this report is put together

Sessions are grouped first by the page they started on, which is why the total matches Google Analytics' own session count rather than only counting homepage arrivals. The step after a starting page is an exact count: sessions that started there and opened that page next. From the third page on, numbers marked ≈ are estimates. Google Analytics' reporting API doesn't hand out full step-by-step sequences (its Path Exploration report isn't available through it), so each deeper step takes the sessions on that branch and applies the share of visitors who moved on from that page to each next one, among sessions that started on the same page. That way each journey thins out step by step the way real visits do, and ends once a step drops below one session.

A + marks a page with a next step: click it to open that one step, then keep clicking the + on the pages beneath to follow the journey as far as you like. − closes a step and everything opened under it. "Collapse steps" closes them all.

Traffic types are Google Analytics' own session channel groups, applied at the source: picking one rebuilds the whole report from that traffic rather than filtering a mixed one afterwards. Organic covers organic search, social, video and shopping. Paid covers paid search, social, shopping, video, display and cross-network. Direct is visits with no known source, such as a typed address or a bookmark. Referral is visits from links on other websites. Other is everything Google Analytics files anywhere else — email, affiliates, text messages, and traffic it couldn't classify; hover the Other button to see what is in it. The five add up to All traffic.

Location is Google Analytics' own country and region for each visitor, worked out from their IP address, and it is applied at the source in the same way: picking a place rebuilds the paths, events and totals from that place's sessions for the period and traffic type on screen. "Browse all locations" lists every country that sent sessions, each opening to its provinces or states; click one to filter by it, or pin it to keep it as a button. The number on each button is its sessions for the period and traffic type on screen. Regions add up to their country, and countries add up to All locations. "Unknown" entries are visitors Google couldn't place. IP-based location is approximate — a VPN, a mobile carrier or a head-office network can put a visitor in the wrong place.

Addresses that differ only by a tracking code (?utm_…, ?gclid=…), a #section link, http/https, www, a trailing slash or capital letters (/wecollab/free-trial and /wecollab/FREE-trial) are counted as the same page. Smaller paths are grouped into "Other pages" so no branch becomes a wall of tiny entries; it's a group rather than one page, so it isn't linked or expandable.

When Google Signals is switched on, Google Analytics withholds rows with very small counts to protect privacy. If that happened for the period on screen, a note appears at the top and some low-traffic steps may be missing. A single location has smaller counts than the whole site, so it happens more often there.
